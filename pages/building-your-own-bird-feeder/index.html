Building Your Own Bird Feeder

Introduction to Bird Feeders

Creating a bird feeder can provide endless enjoyment as you watch various bird species come to visit your backyard or garden. Not only is it a fun project, but it also helps to support local wildlife. This guide will walk you through the steps and materials needed to build your own custom bird feeder.

Materials Required

Basic Materials

  • Wooden boards (pine, cedar, or plywood)
  • Wood screws or nails
  • Wood glue
  • Paint or wood stain (optional)
  • Sandpaper
  • Drill with drill bits
  • Saw (hand saw or power saw)

Additional Materials for Specific Designs

  • Hinges for a roof opening
  • Clear plastic sheets (to create a viewing window)
  • Chain or rope (for hanging)
  • Metal or plastic mesh (to keep squirrels out)

Design Considerations

Types of Bird Feeders

Before you start building, consider the type of bird feeder you want to create:

  • Platform Feeder: A flat platform ideal for multiple birds to feed simultaneously.
  • Tube Feeder: A cylindrical feeder that prevents larger birds and squirrels from accessing the seed.
  • Hopper Feeder: A classic design with a roof to protect the seeds from rain and snow and often features a small chamber that holds seed.

Step-by-Step Guide to Building a Simple Platform Feeder

Step 1: Prepare the Wood

Start by cutting the wooden boards into the following dimensions:

  • Base: 12 x 12 inches
  • Sides: 12 x 5 inches (cut two pieces)
  • Back: 12 x 6 inches (cut one piece)
  • Roof: 12 x 12 inches (cut one piece)

Sand the edges and surfaces to avoid splinters.

Step 2: Assemble the Feeder

Using wood screws or nails, attach the side pieces to the back piece to form a box. Make sure the sides are perpendicular to the base. After securing the sides, attach the base to the bottom of the box.

Step 3: Add the Roof

Attach the roof to the top of the feeder, allowing it to slightly overhang the feeder's edges. Use hinges if you'd like to make a roof that can open for easy refilling of seeds.

Finishing Touches

Paint or stain the feeder to protect the wood from the elements. If desired, apply a clear coat for added durability.

Placement and Maintenance

Finding the Right Spot

Hang or place your bird feeder in a quiet area away from heavy foot traffic. Choose locations that have natural cover, like trees or shrubs, to give birds a safe place to rest before visiting the feeder.

Regular Maintenance

Regularly clean your bird feeder and refill with fresh seed to keep birds coming back. Annually check for signs of wear or damage.

Conclusion

Building your own bird feeder is a rewarding project that not only enriches your garden but also supports your local bird population. With a little time and creativity, you can design a bird feeder that is both functional and aesthetically pleasing.
