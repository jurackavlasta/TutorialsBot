Sourdough Bread Basics

Introduction to Sourdough Bread

Sourdough bread is one of the oldest forms of grain fermentation, cultivated by humans for thousands of years. What sets sourdough apart from other types of bread is its unique leavening process involving wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria. The use of a sourdough starter made from flour and water results in a distinct flavor and texture that is beloved by many. In this guide, we’ll walk you through the fundamentals of sourdough bread making from creating a starter to baking your first loaf.

What You Need to Start

Ingredients

  • Water: It’s important to use filtered or dechlorinated water as chlorine can inhibit yeast activity.
  • Flour: Any type of flour can be used, but for the best results, choose a mixture of bread flour and whole wheat flour.
  • Sourdough Starter: This is a mixture of flour and water which has fermented yeast and bacteria. It can take a week to develop a robust starter.
  • Salt: For flavor and to control yeast activity.

Materials

  • Bowl for mixing dough
  • Kitchen scale for accurate measurements
  • Bench scraper for handling dough
  • Proofing basket or bowl lined with a floured towel
  • Dutch oven or baking stone for baking
  • Sharp blade or lame for scoring the dough

The Sourdough Starter

Creating a sourdough starter involves mixing equal parts flour and water, allowing the mixture to ferment at room temperature. Over time, wild yeast will develop and multiply, causing the mixture to bubble and rise. Feed your starter daily with fresh flour and water to keep it active.

How to Maintain Your Starter

To maintain your starter, discard about half of it before each feeding. If you’re not baking often, store it in the refrigerator and feed it weekly. Each time you take it out to bake, bring it to room temperature and feed it at least 3-4 hours before you start mixing your dough.

Mixing the Dough

To make sourdough bread, combine your active starter with water and flour. Begin by mixing until there are no dry bits of flour remaining. Allow the mixture to rest for 30 minutes to an hour; this is called autolyse.

Kneading and Stretching

After resting, gently stretch and fold the dough every 30 minutes over a period of 2 to 4 hours. This process helps to develop gluten, creating the bread’s structure. After the final fold, let it rise until it has approximately doubled in size.

Shaping and Proofing

Once the dough has risen, turn it out onto a floured surface. Shape it into a tight round by folding the edges toward the center. Place the shaped dough into a proofing basket seam side up. Cover and let it proof for 1-3 hours at room temperature, or overnight in the refrigerator for more complex flavors.

Baking the Sourdough

To bake your sourdough bread, preheat your oven to 450°F (230°C) with a Dutch oven inside. When the oven is hot, carefully place the dough onto a piece of parchment paper, score the top with a knife, and transfer it into the Dutch oven. Cover and bake for 30 minutes, then remove the lid and bake for another 15-20 minutes until golden brown.

Conclusion

Once the bread is finished baking, let it cool completely on a wire rack before slicing. Homemade sourdough bread is a joy to bake and eat! With patience and practice, your skills will improve, and you’ll be able to experiment with different flavors and techniques.
