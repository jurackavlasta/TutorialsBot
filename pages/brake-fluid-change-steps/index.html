Brake Fluid Change Steps

Introduction to Brake Fluid Change

Brake fluid is critical for the safe operation of your vehicle. It transfers force from your foot on the brake pedal to the brake components at the wheels. Over time, brake fluid can degrade due to moisture absorbed from the air, which can reduce its effectiveness. This tutorial will provide you with a detailed guide on how to change your brake fluid, helping to ensure your braking system performs optimally.

Tools and Materials Needed

Tools

  • Wrench or socket set
  • Suction pump or turkey baster
  • Brake fluid (check your owner's manual for the correct type)
  • Container for old fluid
  • Rags or paper towels
  • Safety goggles and gloves

Materials

  • Fresh brake fluid
  • Soap and water for cleanup

Step-by-Step Procedure

Step 1: Prepare Your Vehicle

Start by parking your vehicle on a flat surface and turning off the ignition. Ensure the engine is cool before starting the process. Raise the car using a jack and secure it with jack stands.

Step 2: Locate the Brake Fluid Reservoir

Open the hood and locate the brake fluid reservoir, typically found on the driver’s side. Refer to your vehicle's manual if you cannot find it.

Step 3: Remove Old Brake Fluid

Using a turkey baster or suction pump, carefully remove the old brake fluid from the reservoir. Discard it into the container for old fluid. Be careful not to let any fluid spill on your vehicle's paint, as brake fluid can damage it.

Step 4: Add New Brake Fluid

Fill the reservoir with fresh brake fluid to the indicated 'max' level. Ensure you use the correct type of brake fluid as specified in your owner’s manual (most common types are DOT 3, DOT 4, or DOT 5).

Step 5: Bleed the Brakes

It's advisable to bleed the brakes to ensure no air is trapped in the brake lines. Here’s how:

  1. Locate the bleeder valves on each brake caliper, usually marked.
  2. Starting at the brake furthest from the master cylinder (usually the rear passenger side), connect a clear hose to the bleeder valve and place the other end in a container.
  3. Have an assistant press the brake pedal while you open the bleeder valve. Close the valve before your assistant releases the pedal.
  4. Repeat this process until the fluid runs clear and free of bubbles.
  5. Move to the next brake caliper in the correct order (rear driver's side, front passenger side, front driver's side).

Step 6: Check Brake Fluid Level

After bleeding the brakes, ensure that the brake fluid level is at the proper level in the reservoir. Add more if necessary.

Final Steps

Once everything is complete, secure the brake fluid reservoir cap and lower your vehicle if lifted. Clean any spilled brake fluid promptly with soap and water to avoid damage.

Conclusion

Changing brake fluid is a simple yet essential part of car maintenance. Regular changes ensure your brakes function properly and maintain safety. It's recommended to check your brake fluid every 12 months or as specified in your vehicle manual.
